There is an effect of insecurity that is almost never measured in pesos or crime reports: the things families stop doing. It is not only the real risk of becoming a crime victim; it is the way that fear gradually trims the daily routine of people in the Monterrey metropolitan area.

## What fear makes us stop doing

According to ENVIPE 2025, the everyday activity that the most people in Nuevo León (aged 18 and over) stopped doing in 2024 out of fear of becoming a crime victim was letting the minors in the household go out alone, at 70.7% (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). That says a lot: we are not talking about a luxury, but about something as basic as a son or daughter walking to school without an adult accompanying them.

The list continues with equally everyday activities. 36.7% of the population stopped going out for walks (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León), and 23.8% stopped going to the movies or the theater (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). These are simple activities that fear switches off one by one, and this connects to another figure: only 37% of the population felt safe walking alone at night around their home (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). When even the street in front of your house doesn't feel safe at night, it is no surprise that so many families choose to stay inside.

## The home is still the safe space

Here is the contrast worth highlighting: while the street and public spaces generate distrust, the home remains by far the place where people feel most at ease. The place where the population felt least unsafe was "at home": only 15% said they felt unsafe there (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León).

It is half good news: it confirms that families in Nuevo León still have a space they trust, but it also reveals that this space has become so valuable that many prefer not to leave it.
