Every year, INEGI publishes the National Survey of Victimization and Perception of Public Security (ENVIPE), which measures how people perceive safety in their state, their neighborhood and their daily lives. The 2025 edition for Nuevo León paints a clear picture: insecurity is still the main concern of residents, but it does not feel the same in every space.

## Insecurity, the problem that weighs the most

According to ENVIPE 2025, 71.4% of the population of Nuevo León aged 18 and over considers insecurity the most important problem in their state, far above health, which came second with 32.7% (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). It is a figure that confirms something many families in the metropolitan area already feel in their daily routine.

That perception shows up at the state level as well: 67.8% of the population considers it unsafe to live in Nuevo León (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). When the question is asked closer to home, the number drops but is still significant: 36.7% of the population considers their own neighborhood or locality unsafe (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). In other words, the closer you get to home, the lower the perceived risk, but it does not disappear.

## What people avoid doing

This perception has practical effects. Only 37% of the population feels safe walking alone at night around their home (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). For most people, going for a walk at night near home already takes caution; it is not something that comes naturally.

The contrast is greater in public spaces: the place where people felt most unsafe was ATMs on public streets, at 73.7% (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). By contrast, the place where they felt least unsafe was "at home": only 15% said they felt unsafe there (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León).

## The home, the space where we already feel safe

Here is the paradox worth highlighting: while the street, the neighborhood and the state are perceived as risky spaces, the home remains by far the place where families in Nuevo León feel most at ease. The home is the space we can control, lock, monitor and protect in ways that don't apply to public areas.

That does not mean the home is free of risk. In fact, 27.4% of households in Nuevo León had at least one crime victim during 2024, which is 508,800 out of an estimated 1,859,915 households (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). The sense of safety at home is real, but it is also a reason to reinforce it, not to take it for granted.
