When we talk about security in Nuevo León, we almost always think about how many crimes happen. But there is an equally important question that is rarely asked: how many of those crimes actually go anywhere within the justice system? INEGI's ENVIPE 2025 answers that question with a number worth knowing.

## The first filter: reporting or not

In Nuevo León, during 2024 only 10% of crimes were reported to the Public Prosecutor's Office or the state prosecutor, practically the same as the 9.5% in 2023 (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). Of every 10 crimes that happen, 9 are never formally reported.

Why aren't they reported? The same survey explains it: among the reasons victims give, the most notable are wasted time, at 37.4%, and distrust of the authorities, at 8.2% (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). It is not that people don't care about what happened to them, but that reporting feels like a long process that probably won't change anything.

## The second filter: reporting does not always open an investigation

Here comes the part that surprises even those who do report. Of the crimes that were reported, the Public Prosecutor's Office or state prosecutor opened an investigation file in 72.8% of cases, a notable drop from 85.7% in 2023 (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). Almost 3 in 10 reports do not lead to any formal investigation.

## The result: the hidden figure

If we put both filters together, the full picture looks like this: of all crimes committed in Nuevo León, an investigation file was opened in only 7.3% of cases, down from 8.1% in 2023 (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). What remains (crimes not reported, reported without an investigation file, or unspecified) is what INEGI calls the "hidden figure," and in Nuevo León it amounts to 92.7% of crimes committed (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León).

Put another way: of every 100 crimes, fewer than 8 even reach an investigation.

## So what can you control?

This figure is not a call to resign yourself, but to be realistic about where real control lies. If the justice system rarely gets to intervene, the part that does depend on you carries more weight: preventing, deterring and having your own evidence of what happens at your home or business.

Here, [video surveillance](/en/services/videovigilancia/) plays a different role than the one it is often given. It is not about promising that a camera increases the chances of an investigation file being opened (that data does not exist and we are not going to make it up), but about something simpler: being able to see what happened, when and who was there, without depending on a third party to resolve it for you. A camera documents, and often deters just by being there.
