When we talk about home security, we almost always think about the moment of the break-in: the forced door, the broken window, what was taken. But there is another cost that is rarely mentioned and that falls directly on families in Monterrey and its metropolitan area: the economic cost of living with insecurity, whether or not you have been a victim.

The 2025 National Survey of Victimization and Perception of Public Security (ENVIPE) from INEGI, in its Nuevo León edition, puts concrete numbers on this reality. In 2024, the total cost of insecurity and crime for households in the state was **14.7 billion pesos**. Spread across the people affected, that is an average of **6,498 pesos per person** (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León).

To put it in perspective: **27.4% of households in Nuevo León** (508,800 of the 1,859,915 households estimated in the state) had at least one crime victim during 2024 (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). It is not a distant or exceptional problem; it is more than one in every four households in Monterrey and the rest of the state.

## The cost of not preventing

That cost of 14.7 billion pesos is not an abstract figure: it is the amount ENVIPE estimates for all households in the state as a result of insecurity and crime during 2024. Spread across the people affected, it comes to the average of 6,498 pesos mentioned above. It is a real cost that falls directly on families, whether or not they were victims.

The same survey breaks down where that money went. According to the distribution of the economic impact of crime in 2024, **59.2%** corresponded to economic losses caused by crime, **38.0%** to spending on preventive measures and **2.8%** to health expenses resulting from harm (Source: INEGI, ENVIPE 2025, Nuevo León). Applied to the 14.7 billion pesos, spending on preventive measures comes to about 5.6 billion (our own calculation from the two ENVIPE figures).

In other words: almost four out of every ten pesos that crime costs households in Nuevo León go to protecting themselves, and the rest are losses and expenses that have already happened.

## Prevention, the decision that still makes sense

We don't know how much each household spent installing an alarm system or hiring security, and we are not going to make that figure up. What the official data does show is the other side of the scale: the cost of doing nothing is measured in billions of pesos at the state level and in thousands of pesos per affected person, in addition to the strain that no number reflects.
